The ask in three sentences. NeoStay works today on aggregated statistics and the unit's own acuity policy. To make its forecasts accurate for this hospital — and to prove they are — it needs a pseudonymised extract of past neonatal admissions, the unit's census and staffing history, and the local nursing policy. No names, addresses or record numbers are requested, and no patient-level data is stored in the product itself.
A companion workbook comes with this document. docs/NeoStay-Feature-Inventory.xlsx lists all 95 features of the application, one per row, each marked Have, Partial or Not yet, with the dataset it waits on. Management can change any status in the workbook during the meeting; its summary sheet recounts itself.

Start small if that is easier. D7 and D8 are documents and can be agreed in a single meeting; they unlock configuration immediately. D3 and D4 unlock the staffing work. D1, D2 and D6 unlock the predictive models. Each dataset is useful on its own — nothing has to wait for everything.

What is explicitly not requested

  • Names, addresses, dates of birth, hospital or national identifiers of babies, mothers or staff.
  • Free-text clinical notes, images, or anything from which an individual could be recognised.
  • Payroll, pay bands as monetary values, individual performance records, or sickness reasons.
  • Any data about babies who did not receive care in the participating units.

How much data, and what each amount buys

Volumes are driven by statistics, not by appetite.

The rule behind the numbers. A prediction model needs roughly 10 to 20 outcome events for every variable it considers, plus a separate, untouched period to test on. With about 25 candidate variables (Section 3), that means several hundred completed admissions as a floor, and one to two thousand to produce stable estimates with subgroup results. Time-series forecasting is governed differently: it needs enough repetitions of the seasonal cycle, which for a weekly and annual pattern means at least one, and preferably two to three, full years.
Table 4 — Volume, and what it enables
DatasetMinimum viableWhat that allowsPreferredWhat the extra buys
D1 + D2 admissions and course ~300 completed admissions (about 2 years in a 150-admission unit) A first length-of-stay model with a handful of variables; internal validation only; wide intervals. 1,000–2,000 admissions (5–10 years, or 2 years pooled across branches) Competing-risks modelling, subgroup results by weight band, external validation, stable intervals.
D3 acuity per shift 3 months (~2,700 infant-shifts in a 15-cot unit) Check the classifier against real practice; estimate transition rates between levels. 12 months (~11,000 infant-shifts) Seasonal variation, reliable next-shift prediction, per-branch comparison.
D4 census and flow 12 months hourly (~8,800 rows per unit) Weekday and seasonal pattern; a forecast that beats the naive baseline. 2–3 years Confident seasonality, rare-event behaviour (surges, closures), simulation input.
D5 + D6 establishment and rosters 6 months of rosters Compare recommended with actual staffing; calibrate the skill-mix rules. 12–24 months Robust establishment case; fairness and continuity objectives for the roster optimiser.
D7 + D8 policy and branches Current versions Configure each branch correctly — needed before any pilot. With change history Explains shifts in practice over the period being modelled.

If the data is thinner than the minimum

  • Pool across branches with a hierarchical model, so a small unit borrows strength without losing its own signal.
  • Reduce the variable count deliberately, using clinical judgement rather than automated selection, and report that the model is deliberately simple.
  • Stay with the deterministic method and publish its error. A transparent, honest baseline is better than an over-fitted model.
What "enough" looks like in practice. For a unit admitting about 150 very low birth weight infants a year: five years of D1 and D2, one year of D3 and D6, and two years of D4 would support everything described in the dossier, including per-branch validation.

Format, pseudonymisation and transfer

Practical instructions for whoever runs the extract.

File format

One UTF-8 CSV (or Parquet) per dataset, with a header row and the field names used in Section 4. One row per record; no merged cells; no colour coding; no totals rows. A short data dictionary alongside, naming every coded value.

Pseudonymisation

Replace every patient and staff identifier with a study identifier (NS-000001, ST-0001). The look-up table stays inside the hospital, held by the data owner. Dates may be shifted by a constant per infant if required, provided intervals are preserved.

Transfer

Through the hospital's approved secure mechanism — managed file transfer, a secure shared workspace, or encrypted media. Not by personal email, consumer cloud storage or messaging apps. A checksum for each file, verified on receipt.

Handling once received

Stored encrypted, inside the agreed boundary, accessible only to named analysts, never copied to personal devices, and destroyed at the end of the agreed retention period with written confirmation.

What the hospital gets, regardless of what the models show

  • A written data quality report on its own neonatal records — often valuable in itself.
  • A description of its own demand: census by hour, day and season, and the acuity mix behind it.
  • A comparison of recommended and actual staffing, suitable for an establishment review.
  • Per-branch validation figures, published in full, including where the method performs poorly.
  • The configured application, running inside the hospital's own boundary.

Sign-off and checklists

To be completed at the meeting, so nothing stalls afterwards.

Decisions to record today

  • Pilot unit agreed, with two named champions
  • Data owner named, per branch
  • Governance route started (classification, DPIA, sharing agreement)
  • Policy documents identified and owner named (D7, D8)
  • Extract feasibility confirmed with informatics, with a rough date
  • Hosting model chosen: hospital server, hospital cloud, or managed platform
  • Date set for the next review

Informatics checklist for the extract

  • Field names and coded values follow Section 4
  • Study identifiers replace all patient and staff identifiers; look-up retained by the hospital
  • Period and branches agreed in writing
  • Still-inpatient records included, with a blank discharge date rather than being dropped
  • Data dictionary and extract query saved with the files
  • Checksums generated; transfer through the approved mechanism
  • Known data breaks (system migrations, policy changes) noted with dates
