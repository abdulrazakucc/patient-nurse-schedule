About the model

How NeoStay turns aggregated outcomes into bedside intelligence

A project of Waseem Altaf, MD Neonatologist — clinical direction, outcome data stewardship and methodology oversight.

Data foundation

Built on aggregated 2016–2026 outcomes for Very Low Birth Weight infants — median and interquartile length of stay, disposition and survival — stratified across 10 birth-weight bands and gestational age week by week.

How predictions work

Inputs are matched to the relevant weight and gestational-age bins, then linearly interpolated between neighbouring bins so each gram and week matters. Weight- and GA-based estimates are blended; disposition and survival derive from empirical case counts.

Admission condition

Admission condition (stable → critical) and respiratory support (room air → ventilator) raise the modelled acuity, sharpening the nursing forecast beyond weight and gestational age alone.

Staffing intelligence

Nurse workload follows AAP / AWHONN acuity-based nurse-to-patient ratios, modelling the stay as intensive (1:1), intermediate (1:2) and convalescent (1:3) phases to project nurse-hours, NHPPD and shift demand.

Unit scheduling

The scheduling view aggregates a live census into an immediate acuity-based demand, then rosters the nurses you actually have in hand across the day's shifts — showing each nurse's committed hours over 24 hours and flagging any infant left uncovered.

Safe & private by design

A decision-support prototype — not a substitute for clinical judgment. Every figure is traceable to the underlying dataset, and every calculation runs inside your browser: nothing you type is ever transmitted or stored.

Nurse experience & competency

Staffing is not only about how many nurses are on shift, but who they are. NeoStay grades the nursing workforce using Benner's novice-to-expert stages — the framework behind most NICU clinical-ladder programmes.

Level 1 · Novice

Novice / Advanced Beginner

0–1 year. Learning to recognise patterns and apply rules. Best matched to convalescent feeder-grower infants, and to intermediate care alongside a preceptor.

Level 2 · Competent

Competent

1–3 years. Plans deliberately and manages a full assignment. Suited to intermediate and special-care infants, and to stable intensive infants with support.

Level 3 · Proficient

Proficient

3–5 years. Reads a situation as a whole and anticipates deterioration. Takes intensive 1:1 assignments, including ventilated infants, and precepts novices.

Level 4 · Expert

Expert

5+ years. Acts on deep intuitive grasp. Reserved for the genuinely unstable infant — critically ill and ventilated — and is the level required of the charge nurse on every shift.

Matching nurses to infants

Each infant's minimum level is set by acuity, then raised to Proficient when the infant is ventilated or admitted in critical condition, and to Expert when both are true. A nurse may always cover an assignment below their level, never above it — so a shift's requirements accumulate downward from the expert tier.

Safe skill mix

Headcount alone can hide an unsafe roster. NeoStay caps novices at 30% of the bedside team, pairs every novice with a proficient or expert preceptor, and reports an effective care capacity so a numerically adequate but junior shift is visible as a risk.

Provenance. The competency stages and year bands come from the published Benner framework. The experience weightings, the 30% novice cap and the preceptor rule are transparent modelling assumptions for planning discussion — no validated nurse-scheduling dataset underlies them. They are deliberately easy to change as real rostering data becomes available.

Anatomy of a forecast

What happens between pressing “Generate forecast” and seeing the numbers

Locate the bins

A 720 g, 25-week infant falls in the “701–800 g” and “25 Weeks” bands of the historical outcome tables.

Interpolate

Statistics are blended smoothly between neighbouring bands, so 720 g is treated differently from 799 g.

Blend weight & age

The weight-based and gestational-age-based estimates are averaged into one length-of-stay forecast with its realistic range.

Attach confidence

Every result reports how many similar infants stand behind it — more infants, higher confidence in the forecast.
